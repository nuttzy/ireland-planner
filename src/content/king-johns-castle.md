# King John’s Castle  
*King’s Island, Limerick City*

## Overview  
Dominating the River Shannon in the heart of Limerick, **King John’s Castle** is one of the finest surviving Norman fortresses in Ireland. Its high curtain walls, sturdy towers, and interactive museum tell the story of 800 years of city history.

## History & Background  
- Begun around **1210 AD** on the orders of **King John of England**, the same monarch who commissioned castles at Dublin and Lismore.  
- The fortress replaced an earlier Viking settlement and served as the Norman seat of power in the region.  
- It endured centuries of sieges, notably during the **Siege of Limerick (1690 and 1691)** in the Williamite wars.  
- Extensively restored in the early 2000s, the castle now combines authentic medieval masonry with modern visitor facilities.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **Interactive Visitor Centre** — Multimedia exhibits tracing Limerick’s Viking, Norman, and revolutionary past.  
- **The Courtyard** — Original foundations, siege mines, and reconstructed medieval workshops.  
- **Panoramic Battlements** — Superb 360° views over the Shannon, Thomond Bridge, and the city skyline.  
- **Main Gatehouse & Towers** — Massive twin-tower entrance with defensive features still intact.

## Photo Spots  
- **Riverfront Walk** — Classic profile shot from Clancy’s Strand or Thomond Bridge.  
- **Courtyard Interior** — Arches and shadows within the keep.  
- **Battlements** — Best city panorama; go near sunset for golden light on the walls.  
- **Night View** — Castle floodlit and reflected in the Shannon (tripod recommended).

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: Nicholas Street, King’s Island, Limerick City.  
- Admission includes access to visitor centre, courtyard, and towers.  
- Typical visit: **1–1.5 hours**.  
- Open year-round; check [heritageireland.ie](https://heritageireland.ie/places-to-visit/king-johns-castle/) for times and events.  
- Café, restrooms, and gift shop on site.
