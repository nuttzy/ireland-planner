# Lough Rynn Castle

*Mohill, County Leitrim*

## Overview

- A luxurious historic country-house hotel overlooking **Lough Rynn** on a secluded **300-acre estate**.
- Built in **1832** in Tudor Revival style and substantially expanded in **1889**.
- Although called a castle, this is really a grand 19th-century Irish "Big House" rather than a medieval fortress.
- The estate itself is much older: medieval Irish rulers occupied this land centuries before the present house was built.
- We are staying **one night**, so it is worth treating the castle and grounds as an attraction rather than simply arriving in time to sleep.
- Best plan: **walk the estate and walled gardens, explore the historic rooms, have dinner or drinks in the castle, and enjoy breakfast before leaving.**

## History & Background

### Before the Present Castle

- The estate was once part of the territory of the **Mac Raghnaill clan**, rulers of this part of Leitrim known as Muintir Eolais.
- Medieval records mention a crannóg on Lough Rynn in **1247** and a Mac Raghnaill castle in **1474**.
- Ruins of that older castle survive near the lake, about **500 metres from the present hotel**.
- The Mac Raghnaill lands were confiscated during the 17th-century Plantation and eventually passed to the Clements family.

### The Clements Family

- Nathaniel Clements acquired the Lough Rynn estate in the mid-18th century.
- The present house was built in **1832** for Robert Bermingham Clements, Viscount Clements.
- It was designed as a picturesque **Tudor Revival country house**, rather than a defensive castle.

### Lord Leitrim

- The estate later passed to **William Sydney Clements, 3rd Earl of Leitrim**, commonly called Lord Leitrim.
- His holdings eventually extended to approximately **90,000 acres across four counties**.
- His harsh treatment of tenants made him one of the most controversial landlords in 19th-century Ireland.
- He was assassinated in **1878**.
- The killing became nationally famous and was used by land reformers as an example of the deep conflict between landlords and tenant farmers.
- None of the suspected assassins was convicted.

This is not merely decorative "castle history" — the estate is tied directly to one of the major political and social conflicts of 19th-century Ireland.

### The Victorian Expansion

- After Lord Leitrim's death, **Colonel Henry Theophilus Clements** inherited Lough Rynn.
- He substantially remodeled the house.
- Architect **Thomas Drew** designed the major 1889 addition.
- The work included the spectacular **Baronial Hall**, with:
  - Oak paneling
  - Ornate plasterwork
  - A large inglenook fireplace
  - Decorative ceiling work
- The modern hotel grew out of this 19th-century house.

### From Estate to Hotel

- Much of the enormous Clements estate was eventually transferred to former tenants under Ireland's land reforms.
- The house was largely empty by the late 20th century.
- The Hanly family purchased it in **2001**, restored the property and opened Lough Rynn Castle as a hotel in **2006**.

## Don't Miss

### The Walled Garden

- Probably the **must-see outdoor feature** of the estate.
- Once badly overgrown, it has been extensively restored.
- Part of the garden is still a working kitchen garden.
- Fruit, vegetables and herbs grown here are used in the hotel's restaurant and breakfast preserves.
- The gardens overlook the lake and should be particularly photogenic in September.

### Baronial Hall

- One of the most impressive surviving historic interiors.
- Added during the 1889 expansion.
- Look for the enormous fireplace, oak paneling and elaborate ceiling.
- This is where the hotel delivers most strongly on the "staying in a castle" feeling.

### Drawing Rooms

- Don't just walk from reception to our bedroom.
- The historic public rooms retain antique furniture, fireplaces, woodwork and period details.
- Find somewhere comfortable and spend a little time simply enjoying the house.

### Lakeside Grounds

- The estate sits directly on Lough Rynn.
- Mature woods, lawns and lake views make wandering worthwhile even if we aren't interested in a formal hike.
- With only one night here, an easy walk is a better use of time than rushing off to another minor attraction.

### Medieval Castle Ruins

- The remains of the much older **Mac Raghnaill castle** lie roughly 500 metres from the current house near the lake.
- Don't expect a restored attraction — these are genuine ruins.
- Worth seeking out if the route is accessible during our stay because they connect the estate to its medieval history.

## Dinner & Drinks

### Sandstone Restaurant

- The hotel's main fine-dining restaurant.
- Modern Irish cooking with an emphasis on seasonal and locally sourced ingredients.
- Some produce comes directly from the estate's walled garden.
- **Dinner: approximately 6:00–9:00 PM.**
- Reservations are strongly recommended.
- If our Great Value Vacations package includes dinner here, this is absolutely where we should eat.

### Dungeon Bar

- The atmospheric alternative for drinks or something less formal.
- Tucked into the historic castle rather than being a generic modern hotel bar.
- Worth at least looking in even if we eat in the restaurant.

### Drawing Rooms

- Another option for lighter dining, afternoon tea or simply settling in with a drink.
- Better suited to soaking up the historic atmosphere than rushing back to the room.

## Photo Spots

- **Main lawn:** Classic view back toward the Tudor Revival façade.
- **Walled Garden:** Formal planting with Lough Rynn beyond.
- **Lakeside:** Castle, lawns and water together.
- **Baronial Hall:** Fireplace, oak paneling and historic interior details.
- **Garden-facing façade:** Shows the house more effectively as a grand country estate than the entrance alone.

## Why Stay?

**This looks like a very good substitution.**

Lough Rynn doesn't have the medieval-turret appearance people may picture when they hear "Irish castle," but it offers something arguably more interesting: a genuine historic estate with preserved Victorian interiors, beautiful lakeside grounds and a complicated connection to Ireland's landlord era.

For a one-night stay, the key is **not wasting the castle**.

Arrive early enough to walk the walled garden and lakefront, explore the Baronial Hall and drawing rooms, eat or have a drink in the house, and give ourselves a little time the next morning before leaving.

This should feel like one of the trip's experiences, not simply one of its hotels.
