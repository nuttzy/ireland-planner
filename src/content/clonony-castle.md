# Clonony Castle  
*Shannonbridge, County Offaly*

## Overview  
Tucked beside the River Brosna in the Offaly countryside, **Clonony Castle** is a slender 16th-century tower house with remarkable Tudor links. Its story bridges Irish and English history, connecting the midlands to the court of **Henry VIII** through the **Boleyn** family.

## History & Background  
- Built around **1500–1510**, likely by the **MacCoghlan clan**, who ruled this area of Offaly.  
- The castle came under Crown control and was **granted by Henry VIII to Thomas Boleyn**, father of **Anne Boleyn** (second wife of Henry VIII and mother of Elizabeth I).  
- Two of Anne’s cousins, **Mary and Elizabeth Boleyn**, were reportedly buried nearby after living here in exile following the fall of their family.  
- The tower fell into ruin but was later **restored in the 20th and 21st centuries** by private owners.  
- Now privately owned and occasionally open for guided tours by appointment.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **Three-storey Tower House** — Intact exterior walls and battlements, classic late-medieval proportions.  
- **Interior Restoration** — Period furnishings, fireplaces, and exhibits (when open).  
- **Stone Staircase & Roof View** — Compact yet photogenic climb (by arrangement).  
- **Boleyn Memorial Stone** — Carved slab near the castle said to mark the cousins’ graves.  
- **Surrounding Pastureland** — Peaceful rural views toward the River Brosna.

## Photo Spots  
- **Front Approach Path** — Framed by pasture and low stone wall.  
- **Side Angle at Dusk** — Tower silhouette in warm evening light.  
- **Roofline Details** — Machicolations and corner turrets.  
- **Nearby Field Gate** — Captures the isolated, timeless setting.

## Visitor Notes  
- Location: Off R357 between Shannonbridge and Cloghan, County Offaly.  
- **Privately owned** — open occasionally for tours or local heritage events; inquire via official Facebook page.  
- Typical visit: **30–45 minutes** when accessible.  
- No facilities on site; parking limited on roadside verge.  
- Bring wellies if it’s been raining — approach field can be muddy!
