# Dunluce Castle  
*Bushmills, County Antrim – Causeway Coast*

## Overview  
Balanced on a sheer basalt headland above the sea, **Dunluce Castle** is one of the most spectacular ruins in Ireland. Its broken walls cling to cliffs that plunge straight into the North Atlantic, and its history is every bit as dramatic as its setting.

## History & Background  
- The site was fortified as early as the **13th century** by the **de Burghs (Normans)**.  
- The current castle took shape in the **1500s–1600s** under the **MacQuillan** and later **MacDonnell** clans, who ruled much of the Antrim coast.  
- **Sorley Boy MacDonnell**, a legendary chieftain, held it through fierce battles with English forces.  
- A portion of the castle’s kitchens reputedly **collapsed into the sea during a 1639 storm**, hastening its abandonment.  
- By the 18th century, it stood as the romantic ruin seen today—its surviving turrets and bridge spans now stabilized for visitors.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **Bridge & Gatehouse** — Narrow causeway with Atlantic waves below.  
- **Inner Ward Ruins** — Vaults, towers, and arched windows framing the sea.  
- **North Tower Lookout** — Sweeping views toward Portrush and Rathlin Island.  
- **Archaeological Trail** — Outlines of the 17th-century town that once surrounded the castle.

## Photo Spots  
- **Coastal Path South of the Castle** — Best full-profile shot with cliffs beneath.  
- **Inside Gatehouse** — Framed view looking out to sea.  
- **Golden Hour** — Late sunlight on stone against deep blue water.  
- **Nightfall / Storm Light** — For moody long exposures and wave spray.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: 87 Dunluce Road, Bushmills, BT57 8UY (Northern Ireland).  
- Managed by the **Northern Ireland Environment Agency**.  
- Open daily (check for seasonal hours).  
- Admission applies; guided and self-guided visits available.  
- Allow **45–60 minutes** for the site and exhibits.  
- Facilities: small visitor centre, parking, restrooms, and gift shop.
