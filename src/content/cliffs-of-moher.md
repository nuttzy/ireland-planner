# Cliffs of Moher  
*County Clare – Atlantic Coast*

## Overview  
One of Ireland’s most iconic natural landmarks, the **Cliffs of Moher** rise dramatically up to 214 m (702 ft) above the Atlantic Ocean. Stretching for about 14 km along the Clare coast, they offer sweeping views toward the Aran Islands and Connemara on clear days. The cliffs form part of the **Burren and Cliffs of Moher UNESCO Global Geopark**, preserving both geological heritage and spectacular scenery.

## History & Background  
- Named from an old promontory fort, *Mothar*, that once stood on Hag’s Head at the southern end.  
- The cliffs’ layers of sandstone, siltstone, and shale date back more than **300 million years**, when this part of Ireland lay near the equator.  
- Featured in numerous films, including *Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince* and *The Princess Bride*.  
- The **O’Brien’s Tower**, built in **1835** by Cornelius O’Brien, provides an elevated vantage point at the midpoint of the cliffs.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **O’Brien’s Tower** — Observation tower near the main visitor centre with panoramic views of the entire coast.  
- **Hag’s Head** — The southernmost point, quieter and excellent for photography.  
- **Cliff Walk Trail** — 20-minute loop near the visitor centre or full 18-km route connecting Doolin and Liscannor.  
- **Atlantic Edge Exhibition** — Interactive displays about geology, seabirds, and local folklore inside the visitor centre.

## Photo Spots  
- **View North from O’Brien’s Tower** — Classic shot of the cliffs receding in sunlight.  
- **Hag’s Head Lookout** — Capture the sea arch and crashing surf; best in morning light.  
- **Doolin Trail Section** — Late-day glow over the edge; stay behind safety barriers.  
- **Visitor Centre Green Roof** — Unique angle combining landscape and architecture.  
- **Cliff Edge Birds** — Puffins (in season, April–July) nesting in crevices along the slopes.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: Liscannor, Co. Clare, V95 KN9T.  
- Admission includes parking and access to the visitor centre and viewing platforms.  
- Allow 1½–2 hours; more if walking the extended trail.  
- Weather can change quickly — bring a windbreaker and watch footing near exposed edges.  
- Café, restrooms, and gift shop on site.
