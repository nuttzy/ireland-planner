# Doe Castle  
*Creeslough, County Donegal – on Sheephaven Bay*

## Overview  
Set on a small peninsula almost surrounded by the sea, **Doe Castle** is one of Donegal’s most picturesque tower houses. Its stone walls rise directly from the tidal waters of **Sheephaven Bay**, and its long history is steeped in Gaelic clan rivalry, poetry, and legend.

## History & Background  
- Built in the **15th century** by the **MacSweeney (Mac Suibhne) clan**, who arrived in Donegal as **gallowglasses** — professional Scottish mercenaries serving Irish chieftains.  
- The castle became the **MacSweeneys’ principal stronghold**, controlling the coastline and serving as a maritime defense point.  
- Its tower house and curtain walls were repeatedly attacked and rebuilt over the centuries.  
- Associated with **Gráinne O’Malley** (Grace O’Malley, the “Pirate Queen”) through marriage ties between the clans.  
- Captured during the **Cromwellian wars** in the 1650s and later used as a garrison before falling into disuse.  
- Now managed by the **Office of Public Works (OPW)**; the grounds are open daily and the keep occasionally accessible on guided tours.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **Tower House** — 15th-century keep with four storeys and sea views.  
- **Curtain Walls & Towers** — Surrounded by a tidal moat at high water.  
- **Causeway Approach** — Narrow footpath leading through saltmarsh to the gatehouse.  
- **Waterline Walk** — Short path offering panoramic views of Sheephaven Bay and the Derryveagh Mountains.  
- **Interpretive Signs** — Outline clan history and architectural details.

## Photo Spots  
- **Causeway Gate View** — Classic reflection shot of the castle in still water.  
- **Western Shore** — Full profile with mountains behind.  
- **Inner Courtyard** — Stone texture and defensive angles.  
- **Sunset Over the Bay** — Castle silhouette against golden water.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: Doe Castle, Creeslough, Co. Donegal F92 YX02.  
- Grounds open **year-round** (free entry); occasional guided access to the tower in summer.  
- Limited parking near the gate; short walk to the site.  
- Facilities: small picnic area; cafés in nearby Creeslough.  
- Allow **30–45 minutes** for a full visit and walk.  
- Best visited near **high tide** for reflection photography.
