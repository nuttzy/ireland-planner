# The Rock of Cashel  
*Cashel, County Tipperary – Golden Vale*

## Overview
A spectacular limestone outcrop rising above the plains, the **Rock of Cashel** was the royal seat of the Kings of Munster before becoming a major church complex. Today its skyline—round tower, Romanesque chapel, and roofless Gothic cathedral—makes it one of Ireland’s most iconic sights.

## History & Background
- Legendary site of **St. Patrick** converting King Aengus (5th c.).  
- Granted to the Church in **1101**; shifted from royal fortress to ecclesiastical acropolis.  
- Key structures: **12th-century round tower**, **Cormac’s Chapel** (Romanesque, consecrated 1134), and the **13th-century cathedral** ruins.  
- Sacked in the **1647 Siege of Cashel** during the Confederate Wars; later conserved as a national monument.

## Must-See Highlights
- **Cormac’s Chapel** — Exquisite Romanesque stonework; traces of medieval frescoes (guided access often required).  
- **Round Tower** — Early medieval landmark dominating the skyline.  
- **Gothic Cathedral** — Monumental roofless nave and choir open to the sky.  
- **Hall of the Vicars Choral** — Small museum/space with carvings and exhibits.  
- **Hore Abbey** (nearby) — Photogenic ruined Cistercian abbey below the Rock.

## Photo Spots
- **Approach from Hore Abbey field** — Classic full-profile view up to the Rock.  
- **North lawn** — Layered composition of tower, chapel, and curtain walls.  
- **Golden hour** — Warm light ignites the limestone; blue hour is great for silhouettes.  
- **Town viewpoints** — Framed shots along Ladyswell Street and the R505.

## Visitor Notes
- Address: Moor Lane, Cashel, Co. Tipperary.  
- Tickets required; **allow 60–90 minutes** (add time for Cormac’s Chapel tour).  
- Surfaces are uneven; bring sturdy shoes, and watch wind exposure on the plateau.  
- Facilities: visitor reception, small exhibits, restrooms; cafés in Cashel town.
