# Guinness Storehouse  
*St. James’s Gate, Dublin*

## Overview  
The **Guinness Storehouse** is Ireland’s most-visited attraction, celebrating over 260 years of brewing history. Set within the heart of the original St. James’s Gate Brewery, the seven-storey structure forms a giant glass pint, with exhibits spiralling upward through the story of Guinness — from barley and hops to branding and global culture.

## History & Background  
- Founded by **Arthur Guinness in 1759**, when he famously signed a **9,000-year lease** on the St. James’s Gate site for £45 per year.  
- The Storehouse building itself opened in **1904** as a fermentation plant and was repurposed as a visitor centre in **2000**.  
- The site remains part of the working Guinness brewery complex, now owned by **Diageo**.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **The Brewing Floor** — Immersive displays on ingredients, fermentation, and craftsmanship.  
- **Cooperage Exhibit** — Interactive look at the barrels once built by hand for storing Guinness.  
- **Advertising Archive** — Classic campaigns, from the “Guinness is Good for You” posters to the “Surfer” TV spot.  
- **The Tasting Room** — Multi-sensory space for learning aroma notes before your pint.  
- **Gravity Bar** — The glass-walled rooftop bar offering **360° views over Dublin** — and a perfectly poured pint.

## Photo Spots  
- **Exterior Courtyard** — Brick façades with the “St. James’s Gate Brewery” archway.  
- **Atrium Interior** — Stand at the bottom of the glass-pint atrium looking upward for a dramatic shot.  
- **Rooftop Gravity Bar** — Capture the pint against the skyline; sunset is ideal.  
- **Arthur Guinness Signature** — The floor inscription near the entrance makes a nice detail photo.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: St. James’s Gate, Dublin 8, D08 VF8H.  
- Open daily; tickets recommended in advance (especially weekends).  
- Standard visit: 1½–2 hours including the rooftop bar.  
- The Storehouse is fully accessible with lifts to all floors.  
- Photography encouraged; no tripods.
