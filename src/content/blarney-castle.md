# Blarney Castle  
*Blarney, County Cork*

## Overview  
Best known for the legendary **Blarney Stone**, said to grant the gift of eloquence to those who kiss it, **Blarney Castle** is a striking medieval tower house set amid magnificent gardens. It’s one of Ireland’s most visited attractions, but its scale and natural beauty make it well worth the stop.

## History & Background  
- The current castle dates to **1446**, built by **Dermot McCarthy**, King of Munster.  
- Earlier wooden and stone fortifications occupied the site as early as the 10th century.  
- The **Blarney Stone**, set high in the battlements, has been linked (by legend) to everything from the Stone of Scone to Crusader relics.  
- The castle was confiscated after the **Williamite Wars (1690s)** and later fell into picturesque ruin; the **Jefferyes family** developed the surrounding parklands in the 18th century.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **The Blarney Stone** — Embedded in the parapet at the top of the keep; a short lean backward to kiss it (staff assist).  
- **Castle Interior** — Climb narrow spiral stairs through chambers to the roof.  
- **Poison Garden** — Rare and toxic plant collection with fascinating signage.  
- **Rock Close & Waterfalls** — Shaded walk among druidic stones, ferns, and streams.  
- **Lake Walk & Fern Garden** — Scenic trails around the estate.

## Photo Spots  
- **Front Lawn View** — Tower reflected in the river; best in morning light.  
- **Battlements** — Views over Blarney village and surrounding forests.  
- **Rock Close** — Atmospheric moss-covered stonework and waterfalls.  
- **Poison Garden Gates** — Fun, detailed shots of unusual plants.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: Blarney, Co. Cork, T23 Y598.  
- Tickets required; open daily year-round with last entry mid-afternoon.  
- Allow **1½–2 hours** for full grounds.  
- Café, shop, and restrooms on site; large car park nearby.  
- Best to arrive early to avoid tour-bus crowds at the stone.
