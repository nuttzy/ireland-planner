# Dublin Castle  
*Dublin City Centre*

## Overview
For over 700 years, **Dublin Castle** was the administrative heart of English/British rule in Ireland. Today it’s a working state complex and major visitor site, known for its **State Apartments**, **Chapel Royal**, and **Upper Yard**—plus layers of Viking and Norman archaeology beneath.

## History & Background
- Founded as a **Norman fortress** in the early 13th century on the site of a Viking settlement.
- Served as the seat of government until **1922**, when power transferred to the Irish Free State.
- Much of the medieval castle was lost to time and a **1684 fire**; the present ensemble is largely **18th–19th-century** Georgian/neo-Gothic.
- Ongoing use for **state ceremonies**, presidential inaugurations, and EU events.

## Must-See Highlights
- **State Apartments** — Ornate reception rooms (Throne Room, St. Patrick’s Hall).
- **Chapel Royal** — Neo-Gothic chapel with rich carving and stained glass.
- **Dubh Linn Garden & Upper Yard** — Photogenic courtyards linking the complex.
- **Medieval Undercroft** — Remains of the **Viking/Norman** defences on guided tours.

## Photo Spots
- **Upper Yard** — Symmetry of clock tower and arcades.
- **Dubh Linn Garden** — Spiral lawn patterns with castle façades behind.
- **Chapel Royal interior** — Carved galleries and windows (no flash).
- **State Apartments** — Chandeliers and gilded ceilings (mind staff guidance).

## Visitor Notes
- Address: Dame Street, Dublin 2.
- **Guided tours** recommended for the undercroft + State Apartments; self-guided options for courtyards/gardens.
- Typical visit: **60–90 minutes** (longer if doing full tour).
- Central location—easy add alongside Trinity College, Grafton Street, and Temple Bar.
