# National Museum of Ireland – Archaeology  
*Dublin City Centre*

## Overview  
Ireland’s premier archaeology museum, housing treasures from prehistory through the Middle Ages. Located on Kildare Street, the museum itself is a work of art — a 19th-century Victorian Palladian building filled with gold, bog bodies, and Celtic artifacts.

## History & Background  
- Founded in **1890**, originally sharing space with the National Library and National Gallery before becoming part of the newly independent Irish state’s museum network in **1927**.  
- Designed by architects **Thomas Newenham Deane** and **Thomas Manly Deane**, the same father-son team behind the Oxford Museum of Natural History.  
- Today it forms one of four branches of the National Museum of Ireland, alongside Decorative Arts & History (Collins Barracks), Natural History, and Country Life.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **The Ardagh Chalice** — 8th-century masterpiece of early Christian metalwork.  
- **The Tara Brooch** — Iconic gold and silver brooch from around 700 AD.  
- **The Bog Bodies Exhibit** — Naturally preserved Iron Age remains offering eerie insights into ritual life.  
- **Treasury Gallery** — Gold ornaments from the Bronze Age, including torcs and lunulae.  

## Photo Spots  
- **Exterior on Kildare Street** — Ornate limestone façade; best light in the morning.  
- **Main Rotunda** — Grand staircase and patterned marble floors beneath the glass dome.  
- **Tara Brooch Display** — Subtle lighting ideal for close-ups (without flash).  
- **Museum Courtyard** — Quiet corner for people-free architectural shots.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: Kildare Street, Dublin 2.  
- Admission: **Free** (as of 2025).  
- Typical visit: 1–2 hours.  
- Closed Mondays; check times on the official site before planning your route.  
- Photography: allowed without flash; tripods not permitted.
