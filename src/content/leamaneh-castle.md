# Leamaneh Castle  
*Kilnaboy, County Clare – edge of the Burren*

## Overview  
The haunting ruins of **Leamaneh Castle** stand on the windswept border of the **Burren**. Part medieval tower, part 17th-century manor, it’s famous for its dramatic appearance and its equally dramatic legend — that of **“Red Mary” O’Brien**, one of Ireland’s most notorious women of history and folklore.

## History & Background  
- The original **15th-century tower house** was built by **Toirdhealbhach Donn MacTurlough O’Brien**, a descendant of the Kings of Thomond.  
- In the **1640s**, the family added a large **Elizabethan-style mansion** beside the tower, merging the two structures into one grand residence.  
- **Máire Rua (Red Mary) O’Brien**, widow of Conor O’Brien, became the estate’s most infamous occupant — a fierce and ruthless woman said to have married multiple times and defied English rule.  
- After the Cromwellian confiscations, the castle fell into decline and was abandoned by the 18th century.  
- Today, its shell still looms over the road — ivy-clad, imposing, and deeply atmospheric.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **Tower and Manor Ruins** — Gothic windows and arched doorways now open to the sky.  
- **Roadside View** — The castle is easily seen from the main route through the Burren.  
- **Red Mary’s Legend** — Local tales claim her ghost still roams the ruins and nearby hills.  
- **Burren Landscape** — Karst limestone, hawthorn, and open sky surrounding the site.

## Photo Spots  
- **Opposite Road Verge** — Safest, most direct composition.  
- **Field Edge (with permission)** — Captures full height and both wings.  
- **Morning Mist** — Evocative shot with rising fog over the Burren.  
- **Twilight** — The castle glows orange under sunset light.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: Leamaneh Castle, Kilnaboy, Co. Clare, V95 YP83.  
- **Private land** – no interior access, but clearly visible from roadside pull-off.  
- Short stop only; 10–15 minutes recommended.  
- Combine with **Kilfenora Cathedral**, **Poulnabrone Dolmen**, or **Cliffs of Moher** for a full Burren loop.  
- Use caution if stopping on roadside shoulder — visibility can be limited.
