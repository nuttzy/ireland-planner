# Rock of Dunamase  
*Near Portlaoise, County Laois*

## Overview  
Rising from the plains of County Laois, the **Rock of Dunamase** is an atmospheric ruin of a Norman fortress built atop a steep limestone outcrop. Though little more than walls and gateways remain, its dramatic position and far-reaching views make it one of the most photogenic ruins in Ireland.

## History & Background  
- The site was originally an **early Christian hill fort**, recorded as **Dún Másc** (“Fort of the Plain”).  
- Captured by the **Vikings** in the 9th century, later rebuilt as a Norman stronghold in the **late 12th century** by **Strongbow (Richard de Clare)** or his son-in-law **William Marshal**.  
- Granted as part of the dowry to **Aoife, daughter of Dermot MacMurrough**, in her marriage to Strongbow—linking it to the earliest Anglo-Norman arrivals in Ireland.  
- Destroyed during the **Cromwellian wars (1650s)** and never rebuilt, leaving the haunting skeletal remains visible today.

## Must-See Highlights  
- **Main Gatehouse** — Imposing stone entry arches marking the medieval approach.  
- **Inner Bailey & Curtain Walls** — Crumbled outlines framing views of Laois farmland.  
- **Summit Lookout** — 360° panoramas from the highest surviving walls.  
- **Lower Path & Slopes** — Great perspective back up toward the ruins.

## Photo Spots  
- **Southern Approach Path** — Classic rising perspective as the ruins crown the skyline.  
- **Top Walls** — Panoramic countryside backdrop, especially in early morning or golden hour.  
- **Wide Angle from N80** — Distant view showing the entire rock profile.  
- **Drone/Overlook (if permitted)** — Aerial shots reveal the circular plan and sheer cliffs.

## Visitor Notes  
- Address: The Rock of Dunamase, near Portlaoise, Co. Laois (off N80).  
- Free public access; open year-round with no facilities or staff on site.  
- Parking available at the base; **10-minute uphill walk** to the ruins.  
- Surfaces uneven and steep—good footwear advised.  
- Best visited in daylight with fair weather; no lighting or shelter at the summit.
