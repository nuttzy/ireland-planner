# Carlow Castle

*Carlow, County Carlow*

## Overview

- A striking but compact **13th-century Norman castle ruin** near the centre of Carlow Town.
- Built for **William Marshal, Earl of Pembroke and Lord of Leinster**, one of the most powerful figures in medieval Europe.
- Originally a rectangular stone keep with **four huge cylindrical corner towers**.
- Today only the western wall and two towers remain.
- The reason is memorable: in **1814 a doctor attempting to convert the castle into an asylum accidentally demolished half of it with explosives**.
- **Worth stopping if we're already in or passing through Carlow.**
- Allow roughly **20–30 minutes**. This isn't a full castle-tour experience.

## History & Background

- An earlier Norman earth-and-timber fortification occupied the site before the stone castle.
- The present castle was probably built by William Marshal between **1208 and 1213**.
- Marshal had extensive experience in France, and Carlow belongs to a small group of Irish castles using a French-influenced **four-towered keep** design.
- Similar Marshal-associated castles were built at Ferns, Lea and the original Kilkenny Castle.
- The walls were about **2.7 metres thick**, with timber floors and stone stairs built into the walls.
- The original entrance was at first-floor level, making forced entry much harder.
- When the **Irish Exchequer was moved to Carlow in the 14th century**, the castle became an important administrative centre and was enlarged with a third storey.
- It later passed into Crown ownership and changed hands repeatedly during Ireland's conflicts.
- Cromwellian forces took the castle in **1650**.

### The 1814 Disaster

- By the early 19th century the castle was no longer an important fortress.
- Local physician **Dr Philip Parry Middleton** planned to convert it into a lunatic asylum.
- To create more interior space, he used blasting powder to remove parts of the medieval structure.
- The operation went catastrophically wrong.
- The **entire eastern half collapsed**.
- Unsafe remaining masonry was subsequently removed, leaving the two western towers and wall we see today.

Few Irish castles have a destruction story quite this absurd.

## Must-See Highlights

### The Two Surviving Towers

- These give the best sense of the castle's original scale.
- Imagine identical towers once standing at the other two corners, creating a massive rectangular keep.
- Their broad bases and thick masonry show how seriously defensive this building was.

### Cross-Shaped Arrow Loops

- Look closely for the **cross-shaped firing openings**, particularly in the south-west tower.
- Similar arrow loops appear in parts of Chepstow Castle in Wales rebuilt by William Marshal.
- They're a small architectural clue linking Carlow directly to Marshal's wider castle-building experience.

### The Missing Half

- The empty space beside the surviving wall is almost as important as what remains.
- This wasn't a castle slowly reduced to ruins over centuries—the eastern half survived until one disastrous building project in 1814.

### River Setting

- The castle was deliberately positioned near the meeting of the **Barrow and Burrin rivers**.
- The rivers, marshland and elevated ground gave the medieval fortress natural protection while allowing it to control an important river crossing.

## Photo Spots

- **Front of the west wall:** Best classic view of both surviving towers together.
- **Low angle near the towers:** Emphasizes their size and battered defensive bases.
- **Side view:** Helps show that we're looking at only half of the original keep.
- The castle photographs better when you know to frame the surviving towers as the remnants of a once-symmetrical four-tower fortress.

## Visitor Notes

- **Admission:** Free.
- **Site:** State-owned National Monument maintained by the Office of Public Works.
- **Experience:** Unguided ruin; no furnished rooms or formal castle tour.
- Discover Ireland currently lists the castle as **open to the public on weekdays only**.
- Because access information has varied between sources, verify if our planned visit falls on a weekend.
- This is a short stop rather than a half-day attraction.
- The castle is close to central Carlow, so it works particularly well as part of a town stop rather than as an isolated destination.

## Nearby

- **River Barrow:** Easy to combine the castle with a short walk along the river.
- **Carlow County Museum:** Useful if we want considerably more local history.
- **Brownshill Dolmen:** About 5 km outside town and much older—the enormous capstone makes it a legitimate destination in its own right.
- **Duckett's Grove:** A dramatically different ruined 19th-century Gothic estate north of Carlow; also potentially worthy of its own POI.

## Why Visit?

**Yes, if we're nearby.**

Carlow Castle won't compete visually with places such as Kilkenny, Cahir or Trim. Half the building is gone, there are no grand interiors, and the visit itself is brief.

But what remains is genuinely important Norman architecture associated with **William Marshal**, and the spectacularly ill-advised 1814 attempt to remodel the castle gives it one of the better stories of any ruin we're likely to encounter.

Think of it as a **20-minute piece of medieval history with a terrific punchline**, not a major castle experience.
