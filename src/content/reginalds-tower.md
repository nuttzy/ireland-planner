# Reginald’s Tower  
*Waterford City – Viking Triangle*

## Overview
A stout circular tower marking the heart of Waterford’s **Viking Triangle**. Though modest in scale, Reginald’s Tower is one of the oldest civic buildings in Ireland and now houses a compact museum focused on the city’s Viking and medieval past.

## History & Background
- The site dates to Waterford’s **Viking** settlement (original timber defenses), with the **stone tower** rebuilt and strengthened in the **Anglo-Norman** period (13th century).
- Named for a Norse leader (often rendered **Ragnall/Reginald**), the tower guarded the waterfront and the approach to the walled town.
- Over the centuries it served as **defense work, mint, prison,** and later as a symbol of the city’s heritage; today it’s part of the Waterford Treasures museum network.

## Must-See Highlights
- **Lower-floor exhibits** — Viking artifacts and early town defenses in a tight, well-curated space.
- **Masonry & arrow loops** — Classic Anglo-Norman features visible inside the circular shell.
- **Upper views** — Peek at the quay/riverfront from higher levels (when open).

## Photo Spots
- **Quayside angle** — Stand back along **The Quay** to frame the circular tower against the riverfront.
- **Viking Triangle streets** — Narrow lanes and stonework give you textured context shots.
- **Blue hour** — Evening illumination on the tower works well with a handheld or light tripod.

## Visitor Notes
- Location: Corner of **The Quay** and the Viking Triangle, Waterford City.
- Typical visit: **20–40 minutes** (it’s a small museum).
- Good as a **short stop** if you’re already exploring Waterford; less compelling as a long detour on a castle-heavy itinerary.
- Check local listings / Waterford Treasures for current **opening hours** and ticket options.
