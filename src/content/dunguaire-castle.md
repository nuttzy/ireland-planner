# Dunguaire Castle  
*Kinvara, County Galway – Galway Bay*

## Overview
A classic 16th-century **tower house** with a walled **bawn**, **Dunguaire Castle** stands on a rocky promontory just outside Kinvara. Its squat keep, flag-topped turret, and tidal reflections make it one of Ireland’s most photographed small castles.

## History & Background
- Built c. **1520** by the **O’Hynes (Ó hEidhin)** clan, lords of Aidhne.
- Name derives from **Dún Guaire** (“Fort of Guaire”), recalling **King Guaire of Connacht** (7th c.).
- Restored in the **1920s** by writer/doctor **Oliver St. John Gogarty**, who hosted literary figures (Yeats, Synge, O’Casey), tying the site to the **Irish Literary Revival**.
- Later conserved for visitors; today it opens seasonally and sometimes hosts a medieval-style banquet.

## Must-See Highlights
- **Tower House Interior** — Compact rooms, exhibits, and roof walk (seasonal).
- **Bawn & Gateway** — Stone curtain walls encircling the keep.
- **Shoreline Path** — Short loop around the promontory with changing tides and views to the **Burren**.

## Photo Spots
- **Causeway Approach** — Frontal view with the gate and keep above.
- **Low-tide Reflections** — Mirror shots in the tidal pool at calm water.
- **Burren Backdrop** — Frame limestone hills across the bay in clear weather.
- **Blue Hour** — The keep lit against dusk (tripod recommended).

## Visitor Notes
- Location: N67 just east of **Kinvara** village (parking nearby).
- **Seasonal opening** for interiors/guided access; exterior views year-round.
- Typical visit: **30–60 minutes** (longer if doing the banquet or full interior).
- Exposed to wind from the bay—bring a layer even on sunny days.
